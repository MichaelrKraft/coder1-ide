## Executive Summary

The core problem is real and financially painful — freelancers overpay the IRS by ~21% due to missed deductions, and no purpose-built Schedule C tool dominates the market. However, the full SaaS execution as originally scoped is too capital-intensive for a solo founder at the proposed price point, and the direct competitor set (Keeper Tax, FlyFin, QuickBooks Solopreneur) is established with VC backing. The path forward is to pivot to a productized service targeting one specific niche, validate with 25 paying customers before writing proprietary software, and price at $29/mo or $199/yr to match actual value delivered.

## Target Customer

**Primary persona:** Freelancer earning $30K–$120K/yr via 1099 income, filing their own return via TurboTax or FreeTaxUSA, currently using a spreadsheet or nothing for expense tracking, paying $0 for year-round tax organization.

**Recommended beachhead niche:** Etsy sellers — large active community, complex COGS/shipping/home studio deductions, weak existing Schedule C content, high trigger-event density (first 1099-K at $600 threshold is catching thousands of new filers).

**Pain intensity:** 7/10 overall (9/10 at tax time; 5/10 year-round)

**Budget:** $15–$35/mo or $99–$249/yr; closes easily with ROI framing ("finds $500 in deductions = pays for itself 2x")

**Where they are:** r/Etsy (1.4M), r/freelance (1.1M), r/tax (230K), Etsy Facebook seller groups, Millo.co newsletter, YouTube channels of CPA creators

**Top trigger events:** Tax season panic, first 1099 received, IRS 1099-K threshold change discovery, quarterly tax penalty, income crossing $50K milestone

## Risk Factors

1. **Seasonal churn trap** — The category is inherently seasonal. Year-round retention requires the quarterly tax estimate feature to deliver tangible value outside Jan–Apr. Without this, LTV is too short to build a sustainable business at $29/mo.

2. **AI competitor moat** — FlyFin and Keeper Tax have years of ML training data on freelancer expense categorization. A new entrant's categorization accuracy will be lower initially, generating more support tickets in a high-stakes domain (taxes). The niche-down strategy (Etsy sellers) partially solves this by limiting the expense pattern space.

3. **Pricing floor pressure** — Wave is free. FreeTaxUSA is $15 total. The market has a free/cheap anchor that makes any monthly subscription feel expensive by comparison. The CPA anchor ($375–$600 for Schedule C prep) is the only frame that makes $29/mo feel cheap.

## Competitive Landscape Summary

| Competitor | Price | Key Strength | Key Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Keeper Tax | ~$20/mo | AI deduction scanning, gig worker UX | No CPA filing in base plan |
| FlyFin | $192–$348/yr | CPA hybrid, audit insurance, 2,000+ bank connections | Higher cost, VC-backed (marketing budget advantage) |
| QuickBooks Solopreneur | $15–$20/mo | TurboTax integration, brand trust | Doesn't scale, no invoicing, forces migration |
| Wave | Free | Full bookkeeping + invoicing | No mileage, no Schedule C export, no tax filing |
| FreeTaxUSA | $15/yr (total) | Cheap, accurate Schedule C | Manual data entry only, no year-round tracking |

**Whitespace:** None of the above produce a TurboTax-compatible drag-and-drop import file that works for the FreeTaxUSA user segment (price-conscious, DIY). This is the clearest technical differentiator available.
